Verdict

Same problem, different definitions of "delayed"

  • EDT covers one job: find editors with delayed papers, draft a reminder, and escalate to the Chief Editor. The same job is Steps 05–08 of the MIP Console's first tab.
  • MIP is the broader and more carefully validated tool. It also covers reviewer reminders, EA and author overdues, and editor performance, which EDT doesn't touch.
  • The two tools disagree on three rules: which papers count as delayed, whether Health Check matters, and who receives the email. The same file can produce different recipients and different paper lists in each tool.
  • Proposed approach: use Gayatri's MIP Console as the base. Bring over five things from Daniel's EDT: the delayed-proportion metric, the Chief Editor threshold rules, the Reviewer Invitation Queue video tip, the surname greeting, and the delayed / not-delayed paper toggle.

What happens on the same export

MIP Summary Report for PRP Lead Ayaka Adachi, exported 24 Sep 2026: 279 manuscripts (JDE 200, HEPR 79), 78 of them at an editor stage. Findings
  1. MIP flags 17 papers that EDT misses, and Health Check causes 15 of them. All are JDE and marked "On Track", and include 6 papers with 2+ reviews in and 6 at E3/E7 with no reviewers invited. The other 2 were hidden by a recent reviewer invite, even though the editor had no usable reviews. Relevant rule: CL-5.
  2. EDT flags 1 paper that MIP leaves with the EA. Paper 3771449 has 14 invited, 1 accepted, that reviewer overdue, and no reports in. This confirms the case for adding EDT's overdue check to MIP. Relevant rule: CL-6.
  3. Recipient routing is low-risk. Task Performer Name matched the Handling Editor on every routed paper. Relevant rule: SC-2.
  4. HEPR produced no flags in either tool. Nearly all of it is at an author stage. Run a second, larger journal before signing off.
