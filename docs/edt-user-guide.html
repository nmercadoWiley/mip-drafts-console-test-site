For Daniel and Editor Delay Tracker users

Delay Tracker to MIP Console Guide

Everything the Editor Delay Tracker did now lives inside MIP Drafts Console v2.1. This guide takes you through your usual routine in the new tool: upload the report, see which editors are behind, draft reminders, and send the Chief Editor workbook.

What stays the same
  • One HTML file that runs in your browser. No data leaves your machine.
  • The delayed-editor list with delayed proportion, and a per-editor view of delayed, not-delayed and all papers.
  • The Chief Editor workbook: include editors by count, by proportion, or either, with an Overview sheet.
  • "Dear Dr {surname}" and the Reviewer Invitation Queue video tip in reminders.
  • Copy rich email, copy plain text, and CSV or Excel uploads.
What's new for you
  • Reminders are drafted for every editor in one click, split into what the editor needs to do: invite reviewers, make a decision, or triage.
  • Each email has the editor's address filled in, and .eml downloads open as ready-to-send Outlook drafts.
  • A warning appears if you load the wrong report.
  • Extra tabs for reviewer reminders, EA and author overdues, and editor performance, if you want them.
Your routine, step by step

From upload to Chief Editor workbook

Open the tool

MIP_Drafts_Console_v2.html → "Editor, Triage & EA Drafts" tab

Double-click the file so it opens in Chrome or Edge. Stay on the first tab. The left panel lists every step on the tab; click a card's header to open or close it.

Upload the MIP report

Step 01 · Load manuscript data → first drop zone ("MIP Summary Report")

Drop in the same MIP export you used for the Delay Tracker. In testing, the MIP Summary Report worked in both tools. The file needs the Invited Reviewers, Accepted Reviewers and Version Number columns. If a yellow Heads up warning appears, you've loaded the wrong export.

Optional: also drop the Activity Tracker into the second drop zone. The 10-day check then counts any editor activity, not just reviewer invitations.

In the Delay Tracker, this was "Upload raw MIP". One difference: the console doesn't remember the file. Upload it again each time you open or reload the page.

Narrow to your journals (optional)

Step 02 · Filter manuscripts

Tick the journals you want. You can pick several, as you could with the Delay Tracker's journal buttons. You can also filter by Health Check or by editor. Everything after this step uses the filtered set.

Check the settings, then generate

Step 03 · Set thresholds → Generate all drafts
  • Editor invite grace period: 10 days, the same as the Delay Tracker.
  • Sign-off name: type your name if you want reminders signed by you. Otherwise they use the EA name from the report.
  • For editors across multiple journals: leave this on "one email per editor, per journal". That matches how the Delay Tracker worked.
  • "Every accepted reviewer is overdue" (ticked): these papers go to the editor to invite new reviewers. This is your tracker's rule.
  • "Don't draft manuscripts whose Health Check is On Track" (unticked): tick it if you want your tracker's Health Check rule. See "Why my numbers look different" below before you do.

Click Generate all drafts. Step 04 then shows where every manuscript went, and it should read Accounted for X / X.

See which editors are behind

Step 05b · Delayed editors & Chief Editor escalation

This is your Delayed editors table. It has the same columns: Editor, Journal, Needs action, Current manuscripts and Delayed proportion, sorted with the most delayed first. Proportions of 50% or more show in red. Use Search editor and the journal dropdown to narrow it.

Click Open manuscripts on a row to get your Paper Navigator, with Needs action, No action needed and All current manuscripts views. Each row shows the stage, required task, task performer, the section the paper was sorted into, days since the editor last acted, Health Check and a ReX link.

The Delay Tracker's "Delayed only / Not delayed / All task-performer papers" buttons are the same three views.

Send reminders

Step 06 · Handling Editor drafts · Step 07 · Triage Editor drafts

Every editor who needs a reminder already has a draft. Click an editor's name to open it. You'll see the To: address, the subject, and the email itself, grouped into sections: "Make Recommendation / Decision", "Invite Reviewers" (with the Reviewer Invitation Queue tip), and "Reviews Pending".

  • Copy HTML keeps the table formatting when you paste into Outlook. This was "Copy rich email".
  • Copy plain text gives the subject plus a plain-text body.
  • Download .eml opens in Outlook as a ready draft with the recipient filled in. This replaces "Open in mail client".
  • Download all (.zip) in the card header gives every draft at once.

Nothing is sent from the tool. You still review and send each email yourself in Outlook.

Escalate to the Chief Editor

Step 05b · Chief Editor workbook (bottom of the card)

This works the same as the Sharing Centre. Pick a journal, choose Include editors based on (delayed-manuscript count, delayed proportion, or either), and set the minimums (defaults 1 and 50%). The preview lists every editor who'll be included and why. Click Download Chief Editor workbook (.xlsx).

The workbook has an Overview sheet with each editor, their email, manuscripts needing action, current manuscripts, delayed proportion and inclusion reason. After that comes one sheet per editor listing their manuscripts, the action needed, days since last action, Health Check and ReX link. Every sheet has filters on its header row.

Keep a record (optional)

Step 12 · Draft status export

Downloads your original report with columns added to show what happened to each manuscript. For each one it records whether it was drafted, to whom, the section, the reason it wasn't drafted, and whether the task performer differs from the Handling Editor. Rows are colour-coded. This is useful when someone asks why an editor did or didn't get a reminder.

Worth knowing

Why my numbers look different

The console decides what "needs action" a little differently from the Delay Tracker, so expect more editors on the list. On the test export (Ayaka Adachi's portfolio), the Delay Tracker flagged 9 papers across 7 editors. The console flagged 33 across 23 editors.

Health Check
The Delay Tracker never flags a paper marked "On Track". The console ignores Health Check by default, because "On Track" papers were found waiting for a decision with 2 or more reviews in, or with no reviewers invited. This accounts for most of the difference.
The 10-day rule
The Delay Tracker checks whether a reviewer was invited in the last 10 days. The console checks when the editor last did anything, and applies the 10 days only to papers waiting for reviewer invitations. Papers waiting for a decision or triage action are flagged straight away.
Action types
Instead of one "delayed" flag, each paper goes into a section: Invite Reviewers, Make Recommendation / Decision, Triage, or Reviews Pending (the EA chases reviewers). Only the first three count as "needs action" in Step 05b.
Decision-ready
A paper counts as ready for a decision only when it has at least 2 unique reviews. With 1 review, it's listed under Invite Reviewers.
Want results closer to the Delay Tracker? Tick the Health Check setting in Step 03. On the test export that gives 12 papers across 10 editors. It won't match exactly, because the other rules above still apply.
Your email template has changed. Reminders now use the console's format: the subject "{Journal code} - Action summary for your manuscripts", an action summary, and one table per section, signed "Best Regards". The Delay Tracker's "Papers Pending Your Action" template isn't in v2.1. If your editors are used to it, say so; it can be added back as an option.
FAQ

Common questions

An editor is on my list here but wasn't in the Delay Tracker. Is that a mistake?

Usually not. Open their manuscripts in Step 05b and look at the Section / reason column. Most often the paper is "On Track" but waiting for a decision, or it has no reviewers invited. The Delay Tracker skipped both of these.

Why did I lose my data when I refreshed the page?

The console deliberately doesn't store manuscript data in the browser, so titles and editor names aren't left behind on shared machines. Just upload the report again.

Can I draft a reminder for just one editor?

Yes. Every editor gets a separate draft in Step 06 or 07. Open the one you want and copy it or download its .eml. Use the Step 02 editor filter if you want to generate for only a few people.

Where do I see a paper's "Reviewer invited ≤10 days?" and "Non-delayed reviewers"?

The console uses "Days since last editor action" instead. You'll find it in the Step 05b manuscript view and in the Chief Editor workbook. Invited, Accepted and Reports counts are in each editor's email table.

A paper moved from "Reviews Pending" to "Invite Reviewers". Why?

Every reviewer who accepted is now overdue, so the editor probably needs new reviewers. This is the Delay Tracker's rule, kept on by default. Untick it in Step 03 if you'd rather the EA just chases the overdue reviewers.

Who do I contact if something looks wrong?

Look the manuscript up with "Double-check a manuscript's data" in Step 01. That shows exactly what the tool read from your file. Then check the Step 12 export for the reason it was or wasn't drafted. If it still looks wrong, send both to Nelson Mercado (AI Automation Solutions) or Gayatri.
